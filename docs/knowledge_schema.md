# Knowledge Schema

## Purpose

The goal is not to summarize content.

The goal is to extract reusable knowledge that can be applied across different contexts, audiences, industries, and decision-making situations.

---

## Knowledge Object

### Source
Name of the book, PDF, article, report, or document.

### Author
Who created the source.

### Chapter / Section
Where the idea came from.

### Knowledge Type
Choose one:

- Principle
- Framework
- Mental Model
- Process
- Heuristic
- Observation

### Principle
The main reusable idea.

### Problem Solved
What issue, confusion, challenge, or decision this knowledge helps with.

### Core Insight
The deeper takeaway in simple language.

### Why It Works
The reasoning behind why this idea is useful.

### Framework / Steps
Any repeatable process, checklist, structure, or sequence.

### Examples
Examples from the source.

### Applications
Where this can be applied, such as writing, marketing, product, business, research, leadership, or decision-making.

### When To Use
Situations where this knowledge is helpful.

### When Not To Use
Situations where this knowledge may be inappropriate, weak, risky, or unnecessary.

### Tradeoffs
What you gain and what you may lose by using this idea.

### Related Concepts
Other connected ideas or themes.

### Keywords
Searchable words or phrases.

### Confidence Score
A rating from 1 to 5 based on how clearly the source supports the extraction.

### Importance Score
A rating from 1 to 5 indicating how useful this knowledge is across multiple situations.

1 = niche or minor insight

5 = broadly useful and highly applicable

### Novelty Score
A rating from 1 to 5 indicating how unique, non-obvious, or distinctive the idea is.

1 = common knowledge

5 = uncommon insight that changes how people think

### Supporting Evidence
A short quote or passage from the source that supports the knowledge object.

### Extraction Date
Date the knowledge object was created.
