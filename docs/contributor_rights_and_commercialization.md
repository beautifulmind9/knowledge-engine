# Contributor Rights and Commercialization

## Status of this document

This document records the intended Knowledge Engine contributor-rights model in plain language.

It is a project policy document, not the final legal contributor agreement. Before Knowledge Engine accepts the first external copyrightable contribution into the proprietary core or official extension ecosystem, the operative contributor agreement should be reviewed for the applicable legal context.

## Core principles

Knowledge Engine wants contributors to receive clear credit for meaningful work while preserving the project's ability to maintain, adapt, distribute, relicense, and commercialize the official product over time.

The intended model is:

- submitting work for review does not by itself transfer ownership;
- rejected work remains with the contributor, subject to any rights already granted in public materials or feedback terms;
- accepted copyrightable work requires explicit contributor terms before integration;
- contributor authorship and credit may be preserved even when Knowledge Engine receives broad rights to the accepted material;
- commercialization of the official Knowledge Engine product and official extension ecosystem is controlled by Knowledge Engine; and
- compensation is never implied merely by submission, review, acceptance, or attribution.

## Feedback, ideas, and suggestions

Ideas, feature requests, issue reports, use cases, high-level suggestions, and general feedback may be considered and used by Knowledge Engine without creating an obligation to implement the suggestion, maintain confidentiality unless separately agreed, pay compensation, grant ownership, or give the submitter product authority.

Contributors should not submit confidential or proprietary information through public feedback channels.

## Copyrightable contributions

Examples include code, documentation, substantial written material, original designs, test suites, structured templates, research artifacts, and other original expression that may carry copyright or similar rights.

Before such material is integrated into the proprietary product or accepted as an Official extension, the contributor must agree to the applicable contributor terms.

## Intended rights granted for accepted work

The final contributor agreement is expected to give Knowledge Engine rights broad enough to:

- use the accepted contribution;
- reproduce it;
- modify and adapt it;
- combine it with other work;
- distribute it as part of Knowledge Engine;
- host and operate it;
- sublicense it where necessary;
- relicense the accepted material as part of future product or component licensing decisions;
- commercialize it as part of the Knowledge Engine product or ecosystem; and
- continue using accepted work even if the contributor later stops participating.

The precise legal form of those rights — including whether any rights must be exclusive, assigned, or licensed — must be resolved in the contributor agreement before external copyrightable work is accepted.

## Commercial control

Knowledge Engine retains control over commercialization of:

- the official Knowledge Engine product;
- Official extensions;
- KE Approved commercial distribution;
- hosted Knowledge Engine services;
- paid Knowledge Engine integrations or bundles; and
- other products or services presented as part of the official Knowledge Engine ecosystem.

A contributor may not assume a right to independently commercialize accepted work as a Knowledge Engine product, extension, add-on, or competing derivative merely because they authored or proposed the original contribution.

Separate written authorization may permit specific commercial activity in the future.

## Contributor use and portfolio credit

Subject to confidentiality, security, and the final contributor agreement, contributors should generally be able to:

- say accurately that they contributed to Knowledge Engine;
- link to public contribution records;
- identify public features, RFCs, research, or extensions they worked on;
- include public contribution information in a portfolio, résumé, CV, or professional profile; and
- reuse knowledge, general skills, and non-confidential experience gained while contributing.

They may not publish private source code, confidential architecture, unreleased features, private user information, credentials, security-sensitive information, or other non-public Knowledge Engine material.

The treatment of reusable code or other copyrightable material outside the Knowledge Engine ecosystem must follow the final contributor agreement.

## Pre-existing contributor material

A contributor must identify material they created before the contribution, third-party material, or material subject to another license when that material is included in a submission.

Knowledge Engine should not assume ownership of unrelated pre-existing material merely because it appears in a proposal. If such material is necessary to an accepted contribution, the required rights must be documented before integration.

## Partial acceptance

If Knowledge Engine accepts only part of a larger submission, contributor terms should apply to the material actually accepted and any additional material explicitly identified in the written agreement.

The remainder is not automatically transferred to Knowledge Engine simply because it was reviewed.

## No automatic compensation

Submission, review, acceptance, approval, attribution, or inclusion does not create an automatic right to:

- wages;
- contractor fees;
- royalties;
- revenue share;
- equity;
- employment;
- partnership status;
- reimbursement; or
- future paid work.

Knowledge Engine may later create paid bounties, contracts, grants, employment, fellowships, royalties, revenue-share arrangements, or other compensation models. Those arrangements must be agreed separately in writing.

## Attribution

Knowledge Engine should maintain transparent contributor records where practical.

Credit may appear in:

- Git history;
- release notes;
- RFCs;
- contributor listings;
- extension records;
- public acknowledgements; or
- feature documentation.

Credit does not imply ownership of the Knowledge Engine product, trademark rights, maintainer authority, or entitlement to compensation.

## Third-party and AI provenance

Contributors must disclose meaningful third-party or AI-generated material contained in a submission so Knowledge Engine can assess provenance, licensing, privacy, originality, and integration risk.

A contributor must not knowingly submit material they lack authority to contribute under the intended terms.

## Future contributor agreement

Before accepting external copyrightable work into the proprietary core or official extension ecosystem, Knowledge Engine should adopt a contributor agreement that reflects this policy and receives appropriate legal review.

Until that agreement exists, proposals, discussion, issue reports, research ideas, testing, and prototypes may be reviewed, but copyrightable material should not be merged into the proprietary core as an external contribution.
