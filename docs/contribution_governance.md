# Knowledge Engine Contribution Governance

## Purpose

Knowledge Engine is an open-collaboration community around a proprietary product core.

The project was created by **Taneen Lewis**, who serves as Founder and Product Steward. Community participation is encouraged, but final product authority remains with the Product Steward or with a future stewardship team operating under rules established by Knowledge Engine.

## Governance principles

Knowledge Engine should be:

- open to useful perspectives from many disciplines;
- transparent about how decisions are made;
- careful about intellectual property, privacy, provenance, and security;
- clear about what is public, proprietary, official, experimental, or community-created;
- willing to accept all, part, or none of a proposal; and
- able to evolve without making accepted contributions permanent forever.

## Roles

### Founder and Product Steward

The Product Steward is the final decision-maker for reserved matters and may approve, modify, defer, reject, or reverse project decisions.

Current Product Steward: **Taneen Lewis**.

### Core Maintainers

Future Core Maintainers may review and approve routine technical contributions, uphold architecture and quality standards, and help manage releases. Their authority is delegated and remains subject to the reserved decision rules below.

### Domain Maintainers

Future Domain Maintainers may lead specific areas such as retrieval, research, UX, integrations, evaluation, documentation, or other defined domains.

### Reviewers

Reviewers may provide technical, product, research, security, accessibility, or domain feedback but do not automatically have merge or final decision authority.

### Contributors

Contributors may submit ideas, RFCs, code, research, designs, documentation, tests, extensions, evaluations, or other work through the open contribution paths.

## Reserved decisions

The following remain reserved to the Product Steward unless explicitly delegated under a future written governance structure:

- licensing and intellectual-property policy;
- commercialization and pricing strategy;
- brand, naming, trademarks, and official endorsement;
- changes to contributor-rights terms;
- access to the proprietary core;
- major architecture decisions affecting the competitive core;
- data-security, privacy, and trust boundaries;
- designation of an extension or component as Official;
- removal of official status;
- major product-direction changes;
- appointment or removal of Core Maintainers; and
- changes to this governance model.

The Product Steward retains final veto authority on reserved matters.

## Decision process

Small, routine, low-risk contributions may be handled through normal review once the required contributor terms are satisfied.

Material changes should use the RFC process:

1. proposal;
2. public discussion where appropriate;
3. revision;
4. advisory community vote;
5. maintainer recommendation where relevant; and
6. Product Steward decision.

Community votes inform decisions but do not bind the project.

## Possible decisions

A proposal may be:

- accepted;
- accepted with changes;
- partially accepted;
- accepted as an official extension;
- accepted as an experimental extension;
- deferred;
- rejected;
- withdrawn; or
- superseded by another proposal.

Knowledge Engine is not required to adopt a contribution in the exact form submitted.

## Partial acceptance

A larger submission may contain useful pieces even if the whole submission does not fit the product.

Knowledge Engine may therefore propose to adopt selected parts only. Copyrightable portions are not incorporated until the applicable contributor terms cover the accepted material.

## Reconsideration and removal

Acceptance is not permanent inclusion.

Knowledge Engine may later modify, replace, deprecate, remove, or revoke official status from a contribution or extension because of product direction, architecture, security, privacy, maintenance burden, quality, legal risk, duplication, incompatibility, or other legitimate project concerns.

Where practical, major removals should be documented transparently.

## Transparency

The project should record material decisions through issues, RFCs, release notes, contributor records, or extension status where appropriate.

Rejection explanations may be concise. Common reasons include scope, timing, architecture, security, privacy, provenance, duplication, quality, maintenance burden, or strategic fit.

## Credit

Contributor credit should be accurate and visible without confusing authorship of a contribution with ownership or stewardship of Knowledge Engine itself.

Project-facing attribution should preserve:

**Knowledge Engine — created by Taneen Lewis**

while also acknowledging meaningful community contributions.

## Conflicts of interest

Reviewers and maintainers should disclose material conflicts that could reasonably affect a contribution decision, including a competing commercial interest, close personal or employment relationship, or direct financial interest in an extension under review.

The Product Steward may reassign review responsibility when needed.

## Evolution

This governance model is intentionally simple for the beta and early community stages. It may later evolve into a more formal stewardship team, maintainer council, domain ownership model, or organization structure.

Any such change should preserve clear creator attribution, transparent decision rights, contributor recognition, and the distinction between the proprietary core and public collaboration surfaces unless a deliberate future decision changes those boundaries.
