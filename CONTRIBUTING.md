# Contributing to Knowledge Engine

Knowledge Engine was created by **Taneen Lewis** and is stewarded as an open-collaboration project around a proprietary core.

Community participation is welcome. Participation does not transfer ownership of Knowledge Engine, grant access to the private core, or give a contributor authority over product direction, licensing, branding, or commercialization.

## Who can contribute

Anyone may participate through the contribution paths that are open at the time. Contributions may come from developers, researchers, academics, designers, product practitioners, writers, subject-matter experts, AI/ML practitioners, testers, and other community members.

## What you can contribute

Useful contributions may include:

- bug reports and reproducible test cases;
- feature ideas and use cases;
- formal proposals and RFCs;
- research and evaluation methods;
- UX and product design proposals;
- documentation;
- output templates and workflow ideas;
- knowledge schemas and structured-data proposals;
- prompt and model-evaluation improvements;
- integrations and extension prototypes;
- public SDK, API-client, example, or test-harness improvements; and
- other work that helps Knowledge Engine turn accumulated information into reusable, applicable knowledge.

The proprietary core is not generally opened to contributors. Work that can be developed against public interfaces, schemas, SDKs, examples, or test harnesses should use those surfaces instead.

## Contribution lifecycle

Substantial work should be proposal-first rather than implementation-first.

1. **Discuss** — open an issue or discussion describing the problem, use case, and proposed direction.
2. **Propose** — if the change is material, use the Knowledge Engine RFC process.
3. **Build** — where appropriate, prototype against public interfaces and public-safe test material.
4. **Review** — maintainers and the Product Steward review technical fit, product fit, safety, privacy, provenance, maintainability, and community feedback.
5. **Decision** — Knowledge Engine may accept the contribution in whole, accept selected parts, accept it as an extension, defer it, or decline it.
6. **Integration** — accepted copyrightable work is not merged into or adopted by the official product until the required contributor terms are in place.

Acceptance is not permanent. Knowledge Engine may later modify, replace, deprecate, or remove an accepted contribution.

## RFCs and community voting

Major product, architecture, extension, governance, or compatibility changes should use a formal RFC.

Community discussion and votes are advisory. The final decision belongs to the Product Steward, or to a future stewardship team operating under the governance rules established by Knowledge Engine.

See [Contribution Governance](docs/contribution_governance.md) and [RFC Process](docs/rfc_process.md).

## Rights, commercialization, and compensation

Submitting work for review does **not** by itself transfer ownership of that work to Knowledge Engine.

Before any copyrightable contribution is officially accepted into the proprietary product or official extension ecosystem, the applicable contributor agreement must be completed. The intended rights model is documented in [Contributor Rights and Commercialization](docs/contributor_rights_and_commercialization.md).

Knowledge Engine controls commercialization of the official product and official extension ecosystem. An accepted contribution does not create an automatic right to payment, royalties, revenue share, employment, partnership, or future compensation. Any compensation arrangement must be separately agreed in writing.

## Credit and transparency

Knowledge Engine values visible, accurate contributor credit. Depending on the contribution, recognition may appear in Git history, release notes, contributor records, RFC records, extension listings, or feature acknowledgements.

Contributors may accurately describe public contributions they made to Knowledge Engine. They may not disclose private source code, confidential architecture, unreleased product information, security-sensitive material, private user data, or other information that was not made public by Knowledge Engine.

## Extensions

Public extension tooling may be forked and used to build community extensions under the license that accompanies that tooling. The private Knowledge Engine core may not be copied or forked merely because extension interfaces exist.

Extensions are subject to the [Extension Policy](docs/extension_policy.md), including rules for review, official status, branding, publication, commercialization, security, and removal from the official directory.

## Third-party and AI-generated material

A contribution must clearly disclose material third-party code, datasets, copyrighted content, model outputs, or other externally sourced material that forms a meaningful part of the submission.

Do not submit material you do not have the right to share or license for the proposed use. Do not submit API keys, credentials, private user data, confidential source material, or secrets.

If AI tools materially generated or transformed code, text, designs, tests, research summaries, or other submitted material, disclose that use in the proposal or pull request so provenance and review can be handled appropriately.

## Review outcomes

Knowledge Engine may provide concise review outcomes such as:

- accepted;
- accepted with changes;
- partially accepted;
- accepted as an extension;
- deferred;
- out of scope;
- architectural mismatch;
- security or privacy concern;
- insufficient provenance or rights clarity; or
- not ready for adoption.

A declined proposal is not a judgment on the contributor. It means the proposal is not being adopted into the official product at that time.

## Stewardship

**Founder and Product Steward: Taneen Lewis**

The project may add reviewers, maintainers, domain maintainers, and a stewardship team as it grows. Delegation does not by itself change ownership or the reserved decision rights described in the governance document.

## Legal status during beta

The contribution framework is active for discussion, proposals, testing, and community participation. Before the first external copyrightable contribution is merged into the proprietary core or adopted as an official extension, Knowledge Engine will require a contributor agreement reviewed for the applicable legal context.

This repository documentation records project policy and intended governance. It is not a substitute for legal advice.
